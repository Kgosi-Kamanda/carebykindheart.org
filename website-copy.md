### STORY SECTION

**Eyebrow:** Our story

**Heading:** Built from the inside of the problem

**Body:**
Patricia spent fourteen years working in hospital discharge planning. She knew exactly which patients were going to end up back in the emergency room within a week. Most of the time it wasn't because of the diagnosis. It was because they went home to a house that wasn't ready for them, or a family that didn't know what to do.

She started Care by Kind Heart in Columbus, Ohio, to close that specific gap. The first year, she managed eight clients herself, doing assessments on weekends.

Today the organization serves families in 38 states. The model is larger but the logic hasn't changed. Every client gets a nurse-led assessment. Every caregiver is matched, not just assigned. And every family is served regardless of their ability to pay in full.

We added pediatric care in 2012, when it became clear that medically complex children faced the same shortage of competent in-home care as adults. We added disability support services in 2015.

The four-service model wasn't a growth strategy. It was a response to need.

### PAYMENT OPTIONS SECTION

**Eyebrow:** How families pay for care

**Heading:** We figure out the financial question after we understand the care question

**Body:**
The first conversation with our team is about what you need, not what you can pay. We've designed it that way deliberately, because financial pressure shouldn't shape a care assessment.

After the assessment, a care coordinator walks through every funding source available:

- **Medicare:** Covers skilled home health services after a qualifying hospital stay. Does not cover non-medical daily living assistance.
- **Medicaid:** Covers both medical and personal care for eligible clients. Many states have specific Medicaid waiver programs for seniors, people with disabilities, and children with complex needs.
- **Long-term care insurance:** Most policies cover in-home care. Our team works directly with insurers to document and submit claims.
- **Veterans benefits:** The VA Aid and Attendance program provides funding for eligible veterans and surviving spouses.
- **Private pay:** Clients who pay privately receive the same care, same standards, and same caregiver consistency as those on any other funding source.
- **Subsidized care fund:** For families whose income doesn't support private-pay rates and who don't qualify for sufficient public coverage, our 501(c)(3) fund provides subsidized care. Funded by donations and organizational surplus. No application required — coordinators raise it automatically.

### FAQ CATEGORY 1: GETTING STARTED

**Q: What happens during the first call?**
A: You talk to a care coordinator, not a salesperson. The first call is about understanding your situation — who needs care, what kind, what the timeline looks like. You won't get a quote in that call. You'll get clarity on next steps and a date for a nurse assessment.

**Q: What is a nurse assessment and why does it matter?**
A: A registered nurse visits your home, reviews medical history and physician instructions, observes the home environment, and talks to both the client and the family. The assessment takes 60 to 90 minutes. It produces a written care plan that documents specific needs, risk factors, caregiver skill requirements, and a schedule recommendation. Nothing about caregiver matching happens until the assessment is complete.

**Q: How quickly can care start?**
A: In most situations, we can begin care within five to seven business days of the nurse assessment. For post-hospital discharge situations where timing is urgent, we have an expedited path that can start within 48 to 72 hours.

---

### FAQ CATEGORY 2: COST AND PAYMENT

**Q: How much does home care cost?**
A: Private-pay rates vary by service type and location. Non-medical home care (daily living assistance, companionship) typically runs between $30 and $40 per hour in most markets. Skilled nursing and pediatric care rates are higher. During or after the nurse assessment, your care coordinator provides a written cost estimate specific to your care plan.

**Q: Does Medicare cover home care?**
A: Medicare Part A covers skilled home health services — nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy — after a qualifying hospital stay, when ordered by a physician and provided by a Medicare-certified agency. It does not cover non-medical daily living assistance, companionship, or personal care unless a skilled need is also present. If you're unsure what your specific Medicare plan covers, we'll help you find out before committing to any services.

**Q: What is the subsidized care fund and how do we access it?**
A: Our subsidized care fund is funded by donations and organizational surplus. It exists to cover the gap between what a family can afford and what the care costs. You don't apply for it separately. When a care coordinator reviews your financial situation and sees a gap, they bring it up. If you're concerned about cost before you call, say so at the start of the first conversation. That information only helps us plan better.

**Q: Do you accept Medicaid?**
A: Yes. We work with Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers, including specific programs for seniors, people with disabilities, and children with complex medical needs. Medicaid coverage and waiver availability vary by state. Our team knows the programs in the states we serve and helps families with the application process where needed.

---

### FAQ CATEGORY 3: CAREGIVERS

**Q: Will we have the same caregiver each visit?**
A: Yes, in the typical situation. We assign a primary caregiver to each client based on skill match, scheduling compatibility, and personality fit. That person is your regular caregiver. If they're unavailable, a familiar backup from our team covers — we don't send a stranger. We're explicit about this in our scheduling policies because consistency is the single thing families most often tell us they couldn't find elsewhere.

**Q: How do you screen caregivers?**
A: Three-tier background check: criminal history, sex offender registry, and professional license verification. Every caregiver, every hire. Paid 40-hour orientation before the first client visit. Annual performance reviews tied to client satisfaction data. We also call references and verify employment history, and we don't waive that step regardless of how urgent a staffing need is.

**Q: Can we meet the caregiver before they start?**
A: Yes, and we encourage it. After the nurse assessment and caregiver match, we schedule a 30-minute introduction visit before care begins. This is not a trial run — it's a deliberate meeting so the client, the family, and the caregiver can ask each other questions.

---

### FAQ CATEGORY 4: SPECIFIC SITUATIONS

**Q: Do you serve people with dementia or Alzheimer's?**
A: Yes. Our senior care caregivers include staff specifically trained in dementia care and memory support. The care plan for a client with cognitive decline includes communication protocols for the caregiver, specific behavioral guidance from the family, and closer nurse check-ins than a standard senior care schedule.

**Q: Can you care for a child who is on a ventilator or feeding tube?**
A: Yes. Our pediatric nursing staff includes caregivers credentialed and experienced with technology-dependent children, including those on mechanical ventilators, feeding tubes, oxygen support, and central lines. We do not assign uncredentialed staff to technology-dependent situations.

**Q: What happens when a client's condition changes?**
A: The care coordinator is notified, and a nurse review is triggered. The care plan is updated before the next scheduled visit where possible. For urgent changes — a fall, a sudden health change, a new diagnosis — the 24-hour nurse line is available to active clients.

**Q: Do you provide care in facilities, or only in private homes?**
A: Both. We serve clients in private homes, apartments, assisted living facilities, memory care units, and skilled nursing facilities. The care we provide supplements rather than replaces facility staff, typically focusing on one-on-one time, personal care, and companion support.
