7 Signs It Might Be Time for In-Home Care

Felister Ndugire, Co-Founder · January 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Nobody wakes up and decides their parent needs help. It's usually a slower realization — a stack of unopened mail, a bruise nobody can quite explain, a phone call that felt a little off. By the time most families reach out to us, they've already noticed something. They just weren't sure it was enough to act on.

Here are seven signs worth taking seriously, based on the conversations we have with families across Spokane County every week.

1. The house tells a different story than the person does

Someone can sound completely fine on the phone and still be behind on laundry, skipping meals, or letting prescriptions run out. Pay attention to what you see, not just what you hear. Spoiled food in the fridge, unopened bills, or a change in how tidy a normally tidy home looks are often more honest than "I'm doing great, don't worry about me."

2. Physical signs worth taking seriously

  • Unexplained bruises or falls — even minor ones, especially if there's more than one.
  • Weight loss that isn't intentional, or a fridge that suggests meals have gotten smaller or less frequent.
  • Trouble with mobility — holding onto furniture to walk, avoiding stairs, or a new reluctance to leave the house.
  • Missed medications, doubled doses, or pillboxes that don't match the calendar.

3. Cognitive and emotional signs

Confusion about dates, repeating the same questions, or getting lost on a familiar drive are worth a conversation with a doctor, not just a home care agency. But withdrawal — skipping activities they used to enjoy, avoiding calls, seeming flat or low — is just as important. Isolation and mild cognitive changes often move together, and it can be hard to tell which one showed up first.

4. The financial signs everyone forgets

Late payment notices, duplicate charges, or unusual purchases can be early indicators of trouble managing daily tasks — not just memory changes, but also fatigue, vision changes, or plain overwhelm. Families are often surprised how often this shows up before anything else does.

5. The caregiver in the family is running out of road

Sometimes the clearest sign isn't about the person who needs care — it's about the family member already providing it. If you're the unofficial caregiver and you're exhausted, missing your own appointments, or dreading the drive over, that's real information. Burnout doesn't make anyone a worse family member. It just means the current setup isn't sustainable.

6. A recent hospital stay or diagnosis

A fall, a surgery, or a new diagnosis is one of the most common reasons families call us. Recovery at home often needs more support than people expect in the first few weeks, even for someone who was fully independent before.

7. Your gut has been telling you for a while

If you're reading this because something has been nagging at you, that instinct usually deserves more weight than we give it. You don't need a crisis to ask for an assessment. Most of the families we work with reach out well before things reach a breaking point — and that's exactly the right time.

"Families almost always wait longer than they need to. Nobody wants to be the one who overreacts. But an assessment doesn't commit you to anything — it just gives you real information instead of a guess." — Felister Ndugire, Co-Founder

What to do once you've noticed something

You don't have to solve this in one conversation. A good first step is a free in-home assessment, where someone sits down with your family, looks at the actual daily-living needs, and helps you figure out whether in-home senior care makes sense — and if so, how much of it. There's no pressure and no obligation attached to asking.
