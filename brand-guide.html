Brand Manual

This guide documents how the Care by KindHeart identity should look, sound, and behave across print, web, and caregiver-facing materials. Version 1.0, January 2026.

Brand essence

Care by KindHeart exists to make professional home care feel personal. Everything we design should read as warm but competent: never clinical and cold, never saccharine or precious. We are a home care services company, not a lifestyle brand, and our visual identity should hold both truths at once.

Mission statement

"To help people recover, age, and live with dignity in the place they call home."

Brand pillars

Trained

Every visual and verbal choice should signal competence: consistent grids, real credentials, specific numbers over vague claims.

Present

Warm, human, unhurried. We show people, not stock icons of hearts and hands whenever we can avoid it.

Accountable

Plain language. No hiding behind jargon. Claims are backed by a number, a policy, or a name.

Logo

The primary mark is a two-tone heart formed from overlapping caregiver and client silhouettes, with a gold accent representing the moment of connection. The wordmark pairs with it at a fixed ratio and should not be reset in a different typeface.

Color palette

Our palette is built from three brand hues, each with a working tonal range, plus a warm neutral system for text and backgrounds. We do not use gradients or purple anywhere in the brand, on principle: flat, confident color reads as more trustworthy for a health services organization than decorative blends.

Usage rule of thumb: Harbor Blue carries authority and headers; Kindness Teal is the action color for buttons and links; Warm Gold is reserved for accents, highlights, and secondary calls to action, and should never cover more than 10–15% of a layout.

Typography

Headlines are set in Lora, a warm serif that reads as credible without feeling institutional. Body copy is set in Inter for maximum legibility on screen, including for older readers and low-vision users.

Voice & tone

We are

  • Direct and specific
  • Warm without being sentimental
  • Confident about our credentials
  • Plain-spoken about cost and process

We are not

  • Clinical or bureaucratic
  • Overly emotional or dramatic
  • Vague about pricing or outcomes
  • Reliant on buzzwords or filler phrases

Write with varied sentence length. A short sentence can carry weight. A longer one can hold the nuance of a family's situation, the kind that a single clause can't cover, so let it run when the thought needs the room. Avoid em dashes; use commas, periods, or a colon instead. Avoid stacked qualifiers like "truly transformative" or "compassionate excellence." Say what happened and let the specifics do the work.

UI components

All interactive components use flat brand colors, rounded corners, and a consistent motion language: a 250–400ms ease-out transition on hover and reveal, never abrupt, never longer than half a second.

Accessibility commitments

  • Text and background color pairs meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast in both light and dark mode
  • All interactive elements are reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus ring
  • Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion; animations are decorative, never load-bearing for comprehension
  • Body text does not go below 15px on any breakpoint
